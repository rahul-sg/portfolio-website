I am an AI/ML Software Engineer, working full time while finishing a master's in Computer Science at Georgia Tech with a machine learning specialisation. The projects below are a search system used daily inside a city government, a housing forecasting service I deployed and operated myself, an LLM evaluation framework, a static analysis tool, and an election model. The subject matter has nothing in common. What they share is that none of them was finished when the model worked.

The bugs I remember all lived in the seams: the ETL that feeds a forecast, the cache that serves it, the deployment that has to stay up at three in the morning. Several of these have real users, which is where that lesson came from, because a system that only has to work on my own machine never teaches it. Most of what I learn now comes from pointing three things at each other: graduate coursework, the production systems I work on day to day, and whatever I am building on my own.

If there is a throughline, it is measurement. The records search refuses to answer rather than return its best guess, because a confident wrong answer costs more than no answer. Touse publishes its realised forecast error rather than only a backtest. Cartographer marks every edge as extracted or inferred and ships a view of what it could not determine. I am more interested in knowing when a system is wrong than in the number it reports when everything goes well.

Public Records Search

Semantic search over a municipal archive, for the City of Cupertino

Search and question answering across the City of Cupertino's public records repository: roughly 1.2 million pages, every record type, 1953 to today. Hybrid vector and keyword retrieval with a calibrated relevance gate, relationship graphs built automatically per record type, and plain-English questions answered with citations. It replaced a system that held a single record type and nine years of data; this one spans more than 20 record types across seventy years, and indexes new documents the moment they are filed.

Stack Python and FastAPI · Azure OpenAI embeddings and GPT · Azure AI Search · cross-encoder reranking · BM25 · Laserfiche as system of record · Azure App Service with Entra ID · CI/CD with Application Insights

LLM Evaluation Framework

Honda Research Institute and 99P Labs

A reference-free evaluator for LLM summaries: a domain-aware rubric judge paired with deterministic signals, refined in a loop that decides for itself when to stop. Grand mean 0.845 across seven lectures and three seeds, and 0.81 Spearman against human faithfulness labels.

Stack Python · GPT-5 as generator and judge · domain-aware rubric bank · Chain-of-Density refinement · Streamlit diagnostics · multi-seed robustness and policy ablation harness

505 races, with a correlated simulation
