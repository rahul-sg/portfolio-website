Housing affordability and ZIP-level price forecasting

A housing tool that computes what someone can actually afford across six loan types, and projects where prices in a given ZIP are heading twelve months out, with a confidence band and a published accuracy record.

The system

Five cooperating processes run over PostgreSQL and Redis: a Caddy reverse proxy, a Vite single-page app, a FastAPI backend, a Celery worker, and a Celery Beat scheduler. ETL jobs collect market data on each source's own publication cadence and write to Postgres, and the API serves it read-mostly. Zillow's ZHVI series alone is roughly 15.8 million rows across three home types, and the ZIP centroid table is about 41,000.

What it computes

A maximum home price across six loan types, conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, 5/1 ARM, and jumbo, against the live 30-year rate from Freddie Mac's weekly survey. A readiness score built on debt-to-income that includes the projected mortgage rather than only current obligations. And a twelve-month price projection for a given ZIP with an 80% band.

The forecast, in two stages

A global LightGBM panel model predicts each ZIP's twelve-month-forward price growth from 37 features: lagged local prices and growth rates, US macro series from FRED and Freddie Mac, metro-level supply and rent from Zillow, and election-cycle and seasonality terms, with ZIP and home type passed as categoricals. That sets the endpoint.

A per-ZIP Prophet model then shapes the monthly path toward that endpoint, blended to the LightGBM anchor with a weight that decays from 0.30 near-term to 0.15 at month twelve, so the anchor dominates the level throughout.

Why two models instead of one

The two questions have different shapes. Where prices land in a year is structural, and benefits from seeing every ZIP at once: rate regimes, supply dynamics, regional momentum. How prices get there month by month is local, and benefits from a model fit to one ZIP's own seasonality and noise.

Anchoring heavily to the panel endpoint also suppresses Prophet's known failure mode, which is extrapolating a recent slope linearly after a boom.

Leakage discipline

Lag features are computed within each (zip, home_type) group, so a condo's history can never leak into a single-family lag for the same ZIP. Macro series are joined as-of and backward, so only values published at or before time t are visible when training at t.

Metro supply is joined same-month on purpose: supply at t and price growth from t to t+12 are contemporaneous, so relative to the target it is not future information.

Holding the forecast accountable

Every served forecast writes a row recording what was predicted and when the horizon ends. A daily job backfills the actual price once that horizon arrives and computes signed and absolute error, so the site can publish realized accuracy per ZIP rather than only a backtest number.

Every training run is logged with its panel size, feature count, and duration. Per-ZIP drivers come from TreeSHAP, so a forecast can be interrogated instead of taken on faith.

Operations

Celery Beat matches each source's real publication schedule: Freddie Mac weekly after the Thursday rate release, FRED weekly, Zillow monthly on the 15th, with the panel retraining on the 16th and cached Prophet trajectories refreshed two hours later. A daily freshness check alerts if any dataset passes its staleness threshold. Every ETL write is an upsert on a composite key, so a rerun is a no-op rather than a duplicate.

Status

I built, deployed, and operated this on a single EC2 instance behind Caddy with automatic TLS. The instance is currently shut down and the source is private. I am happy to walk through the code or the model.
